## NVDA — Nvidia
**Thesis.** The picks-and-shovels of the AI build-out. Nvidia sells the full stack (GPUs, networking, CUDA software), and the software ecosystem makes switching costly for customers. As long as hyperscaler capex grows, Nvidia captures the largest share of it.

**Catalysts.** New GPU generations, sovereign-AI demand, inference workloads overtaking training.

**Key risks.** Capex digestion at the hyperscalers, custom chips (TPUs, in-house ASICs), export controls, key-person risk.

**Exit trigger.** Two consecutive quarters of falling data-centre revenue, or gross margin structurally below 65%.

**Review (Nov 2026).** The thesis has held operationally; the stock has been hit by the capex debate and by the November CEO saga. Jensen Huang's reinstatement on 23 November removes the key-person overhang (see journal).

## GOOGL — Alphabet
**Thesis.** The cheapest of the AI platform giants at entry. Search cash flows fund Gemini, TPUs and Google Cloud, and Waymo is a free option. CORE holding: lower beta, strong balance sheet.

**Catalysts.** Gemini monetisation, cloud margin expansion, TPU sales to third parties.

**Key risks.** AI chatbots eroding search share, antitrust remedies, capex intensity weighing on free cash flow.

**Exit trigger.** Search revenue declining year-on-year for two quarters.

**Review (Nov 2026).** Strong contributor since entry, but the October 2026 capex sell-off (a 12.6% one-day fall) showed that even CORE names are not immune when free cash flow is questioned.

## PLTR — Palantir
**Thesis.** The operating system for applied AI in government and industry. Its AI platform turned Palantir from a consulting-heavy business into a scalable software company, and defence budgets give it a durable base.

**Catalysts.** US commercial growth, new defence programmes, profitability supporting index inclusion.

**Key risks.** Extreme valuation, government budget cycles, the 2026 "AI disruption" debate around software.

**Exit trigger.** US commercial growth falling below 30% while the valuation stays at a premium.

**Review (Nov 2026).** One of the three largest sources of selection P&L. The valuation risk materialised in the 2026 drawdowns, but the business case is intact.

## APP — AppLovin
**Thesis.** An AI-driven ad engine (AXON) with best-in-class margins, expanding from mobile games into e-commerce advertising.

**Catalysts.** E-commerce self-serve roll-out, continued margin expansion.

**Key risks.** Platform dependence (Apple/Google policies), short-seller scrutiny, ad-cycle sensitivity.

**Exit trigger.** Evidence that e-commerce advertisers do not renew.

## SOUN — SoundHound AI
**Thesis.** A small-cap option on voice AI in cars and restaurants, sized as a speculative ticket.

**Catalysts.** Automotive design wins, large enterprise contracts.

**Key risks.** Cash burn, dilution, competition from large-model providers offering voice for free.

**Exit trigger.** Would have been cut under today's rules: worst selection P&L in the book.

**Review (Nov 2026).** The clearest lesson of the portfolio: a story stock with no moat suffered most when generative-AI voice became a commodity.

## RTX — RTX Corporation
**Thesis.** Defensive ballast: missiles, air defence and Pratt & Whitney engines, with a multi-year backlog. CORE holding with a beta well below 1.

**Catalysts.** Rising defence budgets in the US and Europe, aftermarket engine demand.

**Key risks.** Engine quality issues, fixed-price contract overruns.

**Exit trigger.** Backlog shrinking for two consecutive quarters.

**Review (Nov 2026).** Did exactly its job: a solid positive return with a beta close to zero, the main source of ballast in a very high-beta book.

## RKLB — Rocket Lab
**Thesis.** The only credible end-to-end space company after SpaceX (launch, spacecraft, components). Neutron opens the medium-lift market.

**Catalysts.** Neutron first flights, defence and constellation contracts, SpaceX's IPO re-rating the whole sector.

**Key risks.** Launch failures, Neutron delays, dilution.

**Exit trigger.** A Neutron programme failure without a funded recovery plan.

**Review (Nov 2026).** The second-largest winner and a top selection contributor. It is now also the largest single source of portfolio risk, which is why I would cap it under today's rules.

## GEV — GE Vernova
**Thesis.** AI's bottleneck is electricity. GE Vernova sells gas turbines, grid equipment and services into a power-demand super-cycle driven by data centres.

**Catalysts.** Turbine backlog and pricing, grid-modernisation orders.

**Key risks.** Wind segment losses, political risk on data-centre build-out (rose after the November 2026 midterms).

**Exit trigger.** Turbine order intake falling for two quarters.

**Review (Nov 2026).** The best position in the book: the largest P&L and the highest selection P&L, with a beta far below the speculative names.

## COIN — Coinbase
**Thesis.** The regulated on-ramp to crypto, with growing revenue from custody, stablecoins (USDC) and derivatives beyond spot trading.

**Catalysts.** US crypto legislation, ETF custody flows.

**Key risks.** Revenue tied to crypto volumes and prices; bitcoin fell by more than half from its October 2025 peak.

**Exit trigger.** Would be reduced under today's rules (speculative cap).

## MARA — MARA Holdings
**Thesis.** A leveraged bitcoin proxy through mining.

**Catalysts.** Bitcoin price, pivot of mining capacity towards AI data centres.

**Key risks.** Halving economics, bitcoin drawdowns, dilution.

**Review (Nov 2026).** Pure beta to bitcoin, and bitcoin went the wrong way. With hindsight, holding both COIN and MARA doubled the same bet.

## HOOD — Robinhood
**Thesis.** The retail-investor super-app: brokerage, crypto, retirement accounts and banking, with a younger client base taking share from incumbents.

**Catalysts.** Net deposits, product launches, international expansion.

**Key risks.** Sensitivity to retail activity and crypto volumes, regulation.

**Exit trigger.** Net deposit growth turning negative.

## UBER — Uber
**Thesis.** The global mobility and delivery network, now solidly profitable, and the natural distribution partner for robotaxi operators.

**Catalysts.** Free cash flow, buybacks, autonomous-vehicle partnerships.

**Key risks.** Robotaxi players bypassing the platform, regulation of gig workers.

**Exit trigger.** A major AV operator scaling without Uber.

## RDDT — Reddit
**Thesis.** Unique human-generated content: an advertising business plus data-licensing deals with AI labs.

**Catalysts.** International user growth, ad tools, new licensing agreements.

**Key risks.** Traffic dependence on Google search, AI answers reducing click-through.

**Review (Nov 2026).** Negative selection P&L: the search-traffic risk proved more important than the AI-licensing upside.
