## Mandate

Life on the Edge Fund is a simulated (paper-traded), long-only portfolio of 13 US equities. I built it in February 2025 as a student project and opened it on 6 March 2025 with about $50,000 at entry prices. It is equity-only by design: no bonds, no cash buffer, no hedges. The aim is to beat the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) over a full cycle by owning the companies I believe capture the most value from three structural themes: **AI infrastructure** (compute, data, power), the **space and defence economy**, and **retail fintech / digital assets**.

The book is deliberately concentrated and high-beta. I accept large swings against the benchmark in exchange for exposure to a small number of convictions, and I measure myself on whether the result beats what that extra risk alone would have delivered.

## Process

1. **Theme first, then the best-placed company.** For each theme I look for the business with the clearest pricing power or bottleneck position, not the cheapest stock.
2. **Written thesis for every line.** Each position has a thesis, catalysts, key risks and an explicit exit trigger (see *Investment theses*).
3. **Risk buckets sized up front.** Positions are tagged CORE (quality compounders, lower beta), GROWTH (high-conviction secular winners) or SPECULATIVE (option-like, small tickets). No single name above ~12% of capital at entry.
4. **Buy and hold, no rebalancing.** The track record is kept clean on purpose: no trades after inception, so every result shown here comes from the initial decisions.
5. **Review against the benchmark at the same risk.** CAPM attribution, a beta-matched QQQ and per-position selection P&L show whether a move came from the market or from my picks.

## Risk framework

At entry, capital was split roughly 12% CORE, 46% GROWTH and 42% SPECULATIVE, spread over 13 names. Because there is no rebalancing, winners are allowed to grow; the dashboard tracks how far weights and risk contributions drift from that starting point.

## What the numbers taught me

The attribution section is the honest part of this site. Measured over the whole period, the extra beta explains most of the outperformance, and the risk-adjusted return (M²) trails QQQ. Split by year, the picture is very different: in 2025 the stock picks delivered a large alpha on top of what beta implied, and in 2026 the same unrebalanced book gave it back during the momentum and AI-capex sell-offs. Below the headline, the dispersion between names is large: GE Vernova, Rocket Lab, Palantir, RTX and Alphabet added value beyond their beta, while SoundHound, MARA, Coinbase and Reddit destroyed it. The speculative sleeve as a whole consumed close to half of the risk budget for a negative contribution after beta.

If I rebuilt the book today I would keep the convictions and change the risk rules: cap the speculative sleeve at 20% of capital, cap any single name at 15% of total risk, rebalance quarterly back to the bucket targets, and pair the equity book with a real diversifier (gold, short-duration Treasuries) as described in the multi-asset view.
