**What happened.** Three shocks in one quarter. In January and February the "AI disruption" trade hit software: new AI coding and agent tools triggered a sell-off that erased around $2 trillion of software market value, and Palantir was caught in it. Bitcoin fell below $70,000 in early February and Coinbase lost about half its value in three months. Then on 28 February the US and Israel struck Iran; oil rose about 50% in two weeks and Brent briefly approached $120.

**Why the fund moved like this.** January and February were the worst stretch of relative performance: the fund fell while QQQ was roughly flat, because the book was overweight exactly the two areas being sold (software/AI applications and crypto). In March, when the war hit every asset at once, the fund fell no more than QQQ.

**What I expected next.** I separated the two problems. The crypto names had become pure beta to a falling asset with no thesis-level catalyst. The AI-infrastructure names, by contrast, were being sold for macro reasons while their order books kept growing. I expected a sharp rebound in AI hardware and power once the oil shock peaked, which historically happens four to eight weeks into such conflicts.

**Multi-asset view.** This is the quarter where a multi-asset book earns its keep: gold hit a record $5,589/oz on 28 January, and an energy or broad-commodity sleeve would have hedged the oil shock directly. I would have run 10% gold, 5% commodities and short-duration Treasuries.
