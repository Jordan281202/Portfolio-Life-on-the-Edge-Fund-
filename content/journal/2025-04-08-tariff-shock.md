**What happened.** I opened the fund on 6 March 2025, in a market already nervous about trade policy. On 2 April the US announced sweeping "reciprocal" tariffs, and within a week the Nasdaq-100 was in bear-market territory. Unusually, Treasuries sold off at the same time as equities, so bonds gave no protection.

**Why the fund moved like this.** With a beta around 1.5–1.7, falling harder than QQQ was the expected behaviour, not a surprise. The speculative and high-multiple names led the fall, while the two CORE holdings (RTX, Alphabet) did what they are there for.

**What I expected next.** No business thesis had changed: tariffs hit margins and sentiment, not AI demand or defence budgets. I held every position. My view was that policy would soften once markets and bond yields forced it, and that high-beta names would lead any rebound. The 90-day tariff pause on 9 April triggered exactly that.

**Multi-asset view.** In a diversified portfolio this was a gold moment, not a bond moment: gold had just crossed $3,000/oz while long Treasuries fell. I would have held 10% in gold and kept fixed income short-dated.
